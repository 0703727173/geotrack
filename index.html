How IP location is determined

IP blocks are allocated to ISPs by regional registries. Geolocation databases map each block to a place using registry records, ISP-supplied data, routing information and latency measurements. The result usually points to the ISP's hub or office, which is why many Ugandan users appear to be in Kampala wherever they really are.

GPS vs cell tower: pros and cons

  • GPS pros: very precise, independent of the network. Cons: weak indoors, slow cold start, drains battery.
  • Cell tower pros: works indoors, fast, low power. Cons: coarse, depends on tower density, controlled by the carrier.
  • Phones combine both with Wi-Fi (A-GPS). You saw this in action: the first fix is a coarse network guess, then it snaps to a precise GPS fix.

Other uses of this technology

  • Fleet, boda-boda and delivery tracking.
  • School bus tracking for parents.
  • Livestock and asset tracking.
  • Emergency SOS and disaster response.
  • Field data collection for health and agriculture.
  • Geofenced alerts, such as leaving a safe zone.

Technologies that enable them: GNSS (GPS and others), BLE/RFID beacons, LoRaWAN IoT trackers, Google Maps and Mapbox, OpenStreetMap, PostGIS, and Firebase or WebSockets for live sharing.

How this app is built

  • Leaflet + OpenStreetMap: interactive map.
  • Browser Geolocation API: live position (watchPosition).
  • Turf.js: distance, area, buffers and geofence maths.
  • Leaflet.heat and Chart.js: heatmap and speed chart.
  • Nominatim: place search and address lookup.
  • GitHub Pages: free HTTPS hosting, required for GPS in browsers.

Privacy and safeguards

Location is personal data. This app collects as little as possible and keeps you in control.
  • Explicit consent: nothing real is tracked until you agree in the consent box and your browser permission prompt.
  • You are in control: the Stop button is always visible in the header, and "Clear all data" wipes everything.
  • Data minimisation: only coordinates, accuracy, speed and time are used. No name, account or device ID.
  • Stays on your device: the trail is held in browser memory only and is lost on refresh. This app has no server and stores no location. Only your light/dark preference is saved.
  • Third parties, only when you ask: the IP lookup contacts an IP-geolocation service (which sees your IP). "Where am I?" and place search contact OpenStreetMap's Nominatim. Map tiles come from OpenStreetMap and Esri.
  • Sharing is deliberate: "Share my position" creates a one-time snapshot link only when you tap it. It is not live.
  • Secure transport: HTTPS is required by browsers for the Geolocation API.
  • Transparency: the status badge shows whether tracking is idle, live or simulated.
  • Law and ethics: tracking another person needs their informed consent, in line with Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 and principles such as GDPR.

Risks to be aware of

  • Location trails can reveal home, work and routines.
  • Misuse for stalking or profiling, and function creep (data reused for other purposes).
  • Data breaches if location history were stored centrally.
